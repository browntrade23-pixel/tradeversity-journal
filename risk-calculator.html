Free futures & CFD risk calculator

How many contracts or lots can I take for this stop? Enter your account, your risk and your stop: the calculator uses each market's dollar value per point, so you never risk more than you planned. Free, no login.

How the position size is calculated

Risk in dollars ÷ (stop size × dollar value per point + fees) = number of contracts. Example: you risk $250 on Nasdaq with a 20-point stop. One MNQ is $2 per point, so 20 points = $40 per contract → 6 MNQ ($240). One NQ is $20 per point, so 20 points = $400: you can't take even 1 NQ.

Why prop firm traders need it

Prop firms close the account when you hit the daily loss limit or the drawdown. Sizing every trade from your stop keeps each loss small and the same size, so one bad trade can't end the evaluation.

Questions

How many MNQ contracts can I trade with a 20-point stop?

One MNQ point is worth $2, so a 20-point stop risks $40 per contract. With $250 of risk you can take 6 MNQ ($240). One NQ is worth $20 per point, so the same stop risks $400 per NQ: too much for $250.

What is the dollar value per point of NQ, ES, YM and Gold futures?

NQ $20, MNQ $2, ES $50, MES $5, YM $5, MYM $0.50, GC $100 and MGC $10 per point (CME standard contracts).

How much should I risk per trade on a prop firm account?

Many prop traders risk a small, fixed part of the daily loss limit or drawdown on each trade, often 0.25% to 1% of the account. Pick an amount that lets you survive several losing trades in a row without breaking a rule.

Does this calculator work for CFD lots (XAUUSD, NAS100, EURUSD)?

Yes. Switch to CFD lots: the calculator uses the dollar value of one pip for 1.00 lot. Brokers differ, so you can change that value to match your broker's contract specifications.
