How the consistency rule works

Best day ÷ total net profit must stay at or under the rule. Example with a 30% rule: best day $900 and total $2,500 → 36%: not consistent yet. You need a total of $900 ÷ 0.30 = $3,000, so $500 more, without making another day bigger than $900.

The plan that passes

With a 30% rule you need at least 4 profitable days (1 ÷ 0.30). Split the target over those days, stop trading for the day once you reach your daily goal, and keep every day under the limit shown above.

Questions

What is the consistency rule in prop firms?

It limits how much of your total profit can come from one day. With a 30% rule, your best day must be 30% or less of your total net profit. If one day is bigger, you must keep trading until the total is large enough.

How do I calculate if I pass a 30% consistency rule?

Divide your best day by your total net profit. Example: best day $900, total $2,500 → 36%, above 30%. You need a total of $900 ÷ 0.30 = $3,000, so $500 more without beating your best day.

How many days do I need to pass with a consistency rule?

At least 1 ÷ rule: 4 days with a 30% rule (the best day can't be more than 30%), 5 days with 20%, 3 days with 40%, 2 days with 50%. Spreading the target evenly over those days is the safest plan.

Do all prop firms count the consistency rule the same way?

No. Most compare the best day with total net profit, some compare it with the profit target, and some only apply it on funded accounts or before a payout. Always read your firm's rules.
